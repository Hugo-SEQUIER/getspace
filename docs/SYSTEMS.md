# Systems

A **system** is a recurring activity, such as a posting routine, an outreach campaign, an SEO routine or a development lane. Its **lab** holds the hypotheses and lessons it tests. Agents and people can try things freely in the lab, because a lab entry is never recalled. Only **verdicts** reach recall: entries promoted to Validated (it works, a rule or tactic) or Invalidated (it does not work, an anti-pattern). The code is `backend/src/space/systems.py`; the website area is `/app/systems`.

## Model

Systems and lab entries are packed workspace items (`space.workspace_items`, migration `0043_systems`), so each change is a revision with its author, and the history of an entry is the list of its revisions. Readings are rows of `space.system_readings` (migration `0046_system_readings`), without revisions; see [Readings](#readings).

| Item | Fields |
|---|---|
| `system` | `name`, `description`, `status` (`healthy`, `needs_attention`, `broken`, `not_measured`, `paused`), `goal_metric`, `goal_target`, `current_value`, `counter_metric`, `source_of_truth`, `review_cadence` (`weekly`, `per_cohort`, `monthly`, `quarterly`), `last_review`, `next_decision` and `playbook_id`, the ID of a document in the Space |
| `lab_entry` | `name` (the hypothesis or lesson in one sentence, at most 280 characters), `system_id`, `domain`, `status` (`testing`, `validated`, `invalidated`, `retired`), `evidence` (`untested`, `suggestive`, `measured`, `owner_call`), `why`, `apply`, `applies_to`, `source`, `captured`, `note` (what this revision changed), `uses` (`used_in`, `at`, `by`), `verdict` and `retries` |

The domains are fixed: Copy & hooks, Social, Outreach & sales, SEO/GEO, Product & positioning, Dev & engineering, Agents & automation, Video and Ops & business. A new entry starts in `testing`. Its status, uses and verdict change only through promotion, demotion and recorded uses; `save_workspace_item` does not accept either kind, and the Space map leaves them out. Full-text search finds systems and entries, not readings. A Space holds at most 5,000 active systems and entries together, and an entry keeps at most 500 uses.

## Readings

`current_value` is one sentence with no history. A **reading** is a dated number a sensor script measured, such as clicks from Search Console or replies from Gmail, so a review can compare week over week and a sensor that stopped reporting shows. `record_system_reading` takes `command_id`, `date` (at most tomorrow), `value` (a JSON number; strings, booleans, NaN and infinity are refused), an optional `unit` (40 characters) and `note` (500), `source` (required, 200) and an optional `metric`, and credits the agent that called it. `record_system_readings` takes `command_id` and `readings`, 1 to 20 readings of one system with the same fields, at most one per date and metric, and writes them all or none, so a sensor's daily run is one call.

A sensor usually measures several numbers for one system on one day: the goal metric, one to three inputs and the counter-metric. `metric` names which one a reading is: a slug of 1 to 64 characters matching `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_.-]*$` (lowercase, so `Replies` is refused rather than stored beside `replies`), such as `replies` or `counter.unsubscribes`. Omitted or null, it is `goal`, the system's `goal_metric`, so a caller written before metrics existed keeps recording the goal. A system keeps one reading per date and metric: a second write for that date and metric replaces it, readings of other metrics on that date stay, and the old number is gone. A retry with the same command ID answers with what it wrote, even after later readings, while at least one of its readings still holds what it wrote (each row keeps the command that last wrote it); the ID reused for another system, date, metric, number or operation is refused. An archived system takes no new reading, and its readings stay listable.

Readings are a time series, not edited content, so they keep no revision history: `space.system_readings` holds one row per system, date and metric (its primary key), and a write is one upsert. An earlier version stored a month of readings as a workspace item, which wrote a retained revision per reading; the owner archive exports every retained revision and restore refuses archives over 10,000 revisions, so fifteen systems with six metrics a day (65,700 readings in two years) would have made a Space unrestorable after about 111 days. As rows, those two years are 65,700 archive entries of about 300 bytes (19 MiB) and no revision. A Space keeps at most 100,000 readings (that rate for three years); the next new date and metric is refused with 413, and replacing a stored one still works. Reading writes of one Space take a transaction lock in turn, so the count is exact. Readings do not move the Space's content revision, so a sensor writing during a restore review does not invalidate it.

`list_system_readings` returns `{system_id, readings, more}` newest first (the metrics of one date in name order), from an optional `since` date, only the optional `metric`'s readings, at most `limit` (1 to 1,000, default 100); `more` says the limit left older ones out. `list_systems` and `list_verdicts` give each system `latest_reading` (`{date, value, unit, metric}`, the latest `goal` reading by date; input and counter readings are left out) and `reading_age_days` (days since that date, never negative), both null when the system has no goal reading.

## Promotion and demotion

Anyone with write access, the person or their agents, can promote or demote; no model judges an entry. Promotion is refused unless the entry is in testing (or holds the other verdict), the evidence is Measured or Owner's call, the source is set and the system is active, and the refusal names what is missing.

Promotion writes a memory through the ordinary memory write (`Workspace.remember`): titled `Verdict: <sentence>`, with the label, system, domain, evidence, why, how to apply and where it applies, and topics naming the system and domain. The memory's packed metadata carries a `verdict` (label, status, evidence, system, domain, entry ID, promotion time), which stays on the memory through later revisions. The entry then records the memory ID, the label, the time and who promoted it. A retried promotion lands on the same memory, because its ID comes from the command. If the entry cannot be written, the new memory is taken back out of recall, so a refused promotion leaves nothing recallable. A promotion that changes the label replaces the memory.

Demotion moves an entry to `testing` (a retry, which adds one to `retries`) or `retired`. It first takes the verdict's memory out of recall with `Workspace.set_memory_recall`, which writes one revision with `recall.excluded=true` and keeps the claims, then writes the entry. The memory and both histories stay; `list_memories` shows the memory as excluded. A verdict cannot be edited or archived: move it back to testing first. A command ID reused for another entry, revision or move is refused.

## Recall

Recall reads only memory, so a testing or retired entry is never returned by `get_context`, and lab entries are not extracted into memory. A verdict is returned like any memory, with a `verdict` object: `label` (`works` or `does_not_work`), `status`, `evidence`, `system`, `domain`, `entry_id`, `promoted_at` and `url`, the link to `/app/systems/{system_id}?entry={entry_id}`. `RecallVerdict` in `backend/src/space/api/schemas.py` documents it.

## Usage and reuse

`record_verdict_use` appends where a live verdict was applied, with the date and the agent that recorded it; `last_used` is the latest use. A verdict counts as reused when a use falls within 30 days after its promotion. Each system's `stats` gives `entries`, `testing`, `works`, `does_not_work`, `retired`, `verdicts`, `reused_30d` and `reuse_share`, the success measure, with a 30% target.

## Interfaces

MCP: `list_systems`, `list_verdicts` (by `system_id` or `domain`), `create_system`, `update_system`, `add_lab_entry`, `update_lab_entry`, `promote_lab_entry`, `demote_lab_entry`, `record_verdict_use`, `record_system_reading`, `record_system_readings` and `list_system_readings`. `update_system` and `update_lab_entry` replace every field, and `record_system_reading` and `record_system_readings` replace a reading on the same date and metric, so they are marked destructive. The [guide](../backend/src/space/guide.py) tells agents to read verdicts before recurring work, record their use, put untested ideas in the lab rather than in memory, and promote only on evidence.

The website calls browser-session routes that are not in the published API: `GET /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems`, `POST /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems`, `POST /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems/entries`, `GET /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems/activity` (the 30 latest lab changes, each with the revision it replaced, optionally for one `system_id`), under `/v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems/entries/{entry_id}` `promote`, `demote`, `uses` and `history`, and `POST` and `GET /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems/{system_id}/readings` (the same request and `since`, `limit` and `metric` query parameters), and `POST /v1/spaces/{space_id}/systems/{system_id}/readings/batch` (the `record_system_readings` request). Archiving uses the item visibility route; readings follow their system.

## Limits

- A verdict memory is a copy of its entry at promotion. Changing the lesson means moving it back to testing, editing it and promoting it again.
- A system with live verdicts cannot be archived: move them back to testing or retire them first.
- Export and restore keep systems, lab entries, verdicts and readings. After the revisions, the export pages carry one `reading` entry per stored reading (`system_id`, `date`, `metric`, `value`, `unit`, `note`, `source`, `recorded_by`, `recorded_at`) of every system that existed at the export's snapshot, read as each page reads them, up to 2,000 a page. Readings do not count toward the 10,000 revision limit; an archive holds at most 100,000, and a reading whose system is not in the archive, or a second one for a system, date and metric, fails validation. Restore writes them after the systems: a new, copied or replaced system takes the archived readings (on a shared date and metric the archived number wins), a kept one keeps its own numbers and gains the missing dates and metrics, and a merge that would leave the Space above 100,000 readings is refused and changes nothing. A restored copy points at the copied system, playbook, verdict memory and entry when they were copied too, and keeps the original IDs otherwise.
